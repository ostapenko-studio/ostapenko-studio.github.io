What the app is — and isn't

Frost Tonight gives estimates of the likelihood of frost at your location, based on third-party weather forecast data (WeatherAPI.com), and aims to notify you the evening before frost is likely. It is an informational tool, not professional advice. Frost is inherently local: microclimates, hollows, slopes, and ground cover mean frost can occur when none was forecast — and a forecast frost may not eventuate. Forecasts can be wrong, and a notification can be delayed or not delivered by your device (for example if it is off, out of coverage, or the operating system suppresses background activity). For valuable or frost-sensitive plants, crops, animals, or equipment, do not rely on this app as your only safeguard — check conditions yourself and consult professional or official sources — your national weather service (for example the Bureau of Meteorology in Australia, the Met Office in the United Kingdom, or the National Weather Service in the United States), or local agricultural advice.

Frost Tonight is not an official warning service, and it does not forecast the weather. It makes no forecast of its own and takes no measurements: it reads forecast data licensed from WeatherAPI.com — overnight temperature, dew point, wind and cloud cover — and tells you what that data means for frost at your place. It does not issue, relay, or substitute for any government weather warning, and no wording in the app should be read as one.
