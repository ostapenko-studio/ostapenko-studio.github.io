Privacy Policy

UV Index, by Ostapenko Studio · Effective 4 September 2026 · Last updated 4 September 2026

The short version

UV Index shows the current and forecast UV index for where you are. It was built to collect as little data as possible.

  • There are no accounts, no sign-ups, no analytics, no crash reporting, and we run no servers. We hold no personal information about you at all.
  • Three things do leave your device, and none of them come to us: your approximate coordinates go to the weather provider to fetch a forecast and to your phone's operating system to turn into a town name; and in the free version, device and advertising information goes to Google to serve a banner ad.
  • Your coordinates are deliberately rounded to two decimal places (about 1.1 km) before they are sent anywhere. UV varies over tens of kilometres, so the extra precision buys nothing and we do not send it.
  • Ads are requested non-personalised, and the app puts Google into limited ads mode before any ad can load — no personalisation, and no advertising identifier used to profile you. There is no consent form, because there is no personalisation to consent to. Buying Remove Ads stops ad requests entirely.
  • Your settings, your cached forecast, your reminder and your home-screen widget stay on your device. Deleting the app deletes them.

The rest of this page sets all of that out in detail, including who receives what, on what legal basis, which countries it goes to, and what rights you have.

Location, in plain terms

The app asks your operating system for your approximate (coarse) position — it never requests a precise fix. Before your coordinates are sent anywhere they are rounded to two decimal places, roughly 1.1 km. That rounding is deliberate data minimisation: a UV forecast grid is far coarser than a kilometre, so the extra digits would tell a third party where you are without telling the app anything it needs.

Location is off until you turn it on. The app cannot read your position until you grant location permission at the operating-system prompt, and you can withdraw that permission at any time in your device settings. Providing your location is not required by law and we do not require it — but if you decline, the app cannot show the UV index where you are.

The app does not track your movements, does not run in the background, and does not build any history of where you have been.

A correction about the town name

An earlier version of this policy said the town or suburb name was resolved "on your device". That was wrong, and we have fixed it. The app asks the operating system to turn coordinates into a place name, and on both iOS and Android that lookup is served over the network: your coordinates are sent to Apple or to Google and a place name comes back. We would rather say so plainly than leave a reassuring sentence standing that the code does not support.

What we never receive

We — Ostapenko Studio — never receive, store or log your location, your device identifiers or your IP address. That is a statement about us, not about the third parties named in the table, who receive what the table says they receive, directly from your device.

Advertising, and the basis for it

The free version shows a single banner ad served by Google AdMob. Ads are requested non-personalised: we do not request ads based on your interests or on a profile of you, and we do not use your location to target advertising.

The app additionally puts Google into limited ads mode, natively, before any ad can load. No advertising identifier is used to build a profile of you, and no consent form is shown anywhere in the world, because there is no personalisation to consent to.

Serving an ad still means Google's advertising SDK stores or reads information on your device and receives device information and your IP address. Google stores a limited amount on the device to detect invalid and fraudulent ad traffic, and uses the IP address to deliver the ad. Our legal basis for this is legitimate interests: keeping the app free for people who do not buy Remove Ads. You can object to it, and Remove Ads ends it outright.

Google also states that it may estimate your general location from your IP address for advertising purposes. That estimation is Google's, is separate from the coordinates described above, and happens whether or not you have granted the app location permission.

You are in control of this:

  • You can object to this processing at any time — write to us using the details at the end of this policy.
  • Your device's own advertising controls (reset or limit the advertising identifier) apply as well.
  • Buying Remove Ads stops ad requests entirely — the ad SDK makes no further requests, and Google's collection through this app stops with it.

When you use this app, the advertising provider may collect information about your activity over time and across different apps and services. We do not do this ourselves and we receive nothing from it except aggregate revenue.

Do we sell or share your information?

Stated as fact rather than as a legal conclusion: we receive no personal information about you, so there is nothing for us to sell. Ads are requested non-personalised. The advertising provider receives device information, your IP address and an advertising identifier directly from your device, on the basis described above. We receive only aggregate revenue. We do not receive money in exchange for handing over information about you — we receive advertising revenue.

Do Not Track

The app is not a web browser and receives no "Do Not Track" browser signal, so there is no such signal for it to respond to. We do not track you across other apps or websites. The controls that actually work here are the ones listed above: your device's advertising settings, and Remove Ads.

Purchases

The one-time Remove Ads purchase is processed entirely by Apple's App Store or Google Play, who are the merchant of record. We never see your name, payment details or billing information. We receive from the store only a confirmation that the purchase exists, which is what lets the app hide the banner and restore your purchase on your other devices.

Notifications and the widget

The optional daily UV reminder is scheduled locally on your device by the operating system. No notification data leaves your device, and we have no ability to send you push messages. The home-screen widget is drawn from the forecast already cached on your device.

How long anything is kept, and how it is destroyed

  • We retain nothing. There is no server, no database and no log file on our side, so there is nothing for us to keep, and processing by us ends when you close the app.
  • On your device: your settings persist until you change them; the cached forecast is overwritten each time the app refreshes. Both are destroyed, along with everything else the app stores, when you delete the app — which is the deletion mechanism for all app-held data, and it is entirely in your hands.
  • Third parties retain what they retain under their own policies, linked in the table above.

Security

Our security measures are modest because our exposure is modest, and we would rather describe them than imply more. Every request the app makes travels over HTTPS. We operate no server and hold no personal information, so there is no store of user data to breach on our side. There are no accounts and no passwords. Data on your device is protected by your device's own operating-system protections.

Automated decisions

There is no automated decision-making producing legal effects or similarly significant effects on you, and no profiling. The app calculates a UV band from a forecast number and, if you have set a skin type, shifts that band — that is arithmetic shown to you on screen, not a decision made about you.

We are not permitted to collect additional information from you purely to try to find you in data we do not have. Our minimum age position is that the app is intended for people aged 13 and over, and in jurisdictions that set a higher threshold for consent — including Brazil, which covers adolescents as well as children, and India — anyone below that threshold should use the app only with the involvement of a parent or guardian.

Because the app is used by young people in the sun, we hold ourselves to this regardless of who is using it:
